# Session 4 - Skewness (Basic)

## Task 1: Swiggy Daily Orders

Daily order counts: [12, 15, 17, 14, 13, 16, 200, 18, 14, 15]

- Mean: 33.4
- Median: 15
- Distribution: Right-skewed

Explanation: The unusually high value of 200 orders pulls the mean upward, making it greater than the median. This creates a long tail on the right side.

## Task 2: Instagram Influencer Followers

Follower counts (in thousands): [5, 7, 8, 8, 9, 10, 12, 15, 95]

- Mean: 18.78 thousand
- Median: 9 thousand
- Distribution: Right-skewed

Explanation: The influencer with 95 thousand followers pulls the mean above the median, indicating positive or right skewness.

## Task 3: Real-World Example of Right-Skewed Data

Example: YouTube video views.

Dataset: [100, 150, 200, 250, 300, 500, 1000, 10000]

Explanation: Most videos have relatively low views, while a few videos receive very high views. These high values create a long tail on the right side, so the data is right-skewed.

## Task 4: Flipkart Product Ratings

Ratings: [3, 3, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 5, 5, 5]

Answer: The distribution is left-skewed (negatively skewed).

Explanation: Most ratings are concentrated at 5, while fewer ratings extend toward the lower values, creating a tail on the left.
